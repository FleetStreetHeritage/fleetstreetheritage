Child’s Bank (No. 1) Dignified High Renaissance Portland stone façade with giant Corinthian order, balustrade and arched ground floor. Historic NatWest signage retained. 1878 by John Gibson. Grade II*.

S. Weingott & Son (No. 3) 1912 refronting of a C18 house for tobacconists S.

Weingott & Son. Large metal-framed shop windows contrast with traditional quoins. Grade II.

Middle T emple Gateway Elegant 1684 gateway by Roger North in red brick and stone with giant Ionic pilasters, balconies and a dentilled pediment. Grade I.

Legal & General Assurance (No. 10) Brick and terracotta in a loosely Flemish style with an asymmetrical façade. 1885 by R. W . Edis. Grade II.

Union Bank of London (No. 13–14) Dignified Neoclassical stone façade with Doric ground floor and balanced tripartite composition. 1856 by G.

Aitchison Sr. Grade II.

Wildy & Sons (No. 15–16) Narrow but ornate frontage with a distinctive Venetian window. 1856 by J. H. Stevens.

Inner T emple Gateway (Prince Henry’s Room – No. 17) Half-timbered gateway of 1610–11. Despite later alterations, it remains a rare survivor of pre-Great Fire architecture. Grade II*.

Goslings & Sharpe (No. 18–19) Renaissance Revival bank with strong Classical detailing, window aprons and pediments. Now owned by Barclays.

1898 by A. C. Blomfield. Grade II.

London & Provincial Life Assurance (No. 21) Ornate Italianate Renaissance Revival with carved decoration. 1853 by J. Shaw Jr. Grade II.

Ye Olde Cock Tavern (No. 22) T udor Revival frontage added in 1912 after street widening. Grade II.

T emple Bar House (No. 23–28) Classical design with Baroque detailing, granite ground floor and blocked upper storeys. 1902.

Promoter Insurance (No. 29) High Victorian Eclectic style with varied storeys, stone dressings and arched attic windows. 1860 by W . G.

Bartleet. Grade II.

Messrs Philip (No. 30–32) Portland stone bays with historicist detailing and decorative window canopies. 1883 by T . E. Knightley.

Grade II.

Plain Georgian brick house, home to Murray’s Publishers from 1768–93. Grade II.

Hoare’s Bank (No. 37) Refined Classical bank with warm sandstone façade and elegant interiors. Still occupied by Hoare & Co. 1829 by C. Parker. Grade II*.

No. 40–43 Neoclassical tripartite façade with Ionic pilasters and simplified attic storeys. 1913 by O. C. Wylson.

Mitre House (No. 44–45) Stripped Classical with subtle Art Deco touches. 1929 by M. K. Matthews.

London News Agency (No. 46) Simple brick building with large windows and original London News Agency lettering.

El Vino Wine Bar (No. 47) Historic wine bar in a plain Edwardian building. Original sign and interiors survive. Inspiration for Rumpole of the Bailey’s Pomeroy’s.

Norwich Union Insurance (No. 49–50) Grand Edwardian Renaissance Revival with arches, screening colonnade and balustrade. 1911–12 by J. M.

Brooks. Grade II.

Colourful narrow façade with patterned red and green brickwork and Gothic-style bay windows. c.1905.

Clifford Milburn & Co. (No. 54) Vertical emphasis created by chamfered uprights and plain aprons. 1927 by T rehearne & Norman.

Three-bay brick and stone building with restrained Classical detailing. A conservative 1930s design.
