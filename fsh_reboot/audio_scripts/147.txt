Glasgow Herald (No.56–57) Tall, narrow frontage with post-Classical details applied in an Art Deco manner. Granite and gilt ground floor followed by a three-storey bow window, mezzanine and attic storeys. Built in 1927 by T ubbs, Son & Duncan.

Grade II listed.

No.58–59 Late C19 or early C20 renditions of traditional forms. The Queen Anne details and proportions of No.58 outshine those of No.59. Now combined as a single building.

Associated with C18 clock and scientific instrument maker George Adams and his successors. Above the lower two storeys it retains Georgian forms, though probably rebuilt later.

J Lyons Café (No.61) Former tea shop retaining formal, well-executed Baroque Revival detailing typical of quality Edwardian buildings.

Built in 1910, probably by Lyons’ in-house architects.

(claxity.com) JUNCTION WITH BOUVERIE STREET The Scotsman (No.63) A dignified example of post-war stripped Classicism.

The prominent fourth-storey cornice and wrought-iron balconies stand out, while sculptural details recall the original occupant. Built in 1921 by Frank Matcham.

(claxity.com) No.64–65 Asymmetrical Postmodern building with a large arch opening onto a courtyard. Contrasting dark lower storeys, lighter middle storeys and a recessed attic create a balanced composition. Built in 1988 by YRM Partnership.

The Tipperary (No.66) Post-Fire building (foundation laid 1667), re-clad with new interiors in 1895. Grade II listed.

Rounded corner with restrained four-storey cladding and attic. Built in 1926 by A. A. H. Scott.

JUNCTION WITH WHITEFRIARS STREET The Fleet Street frontage from Nos.68–85, including Barclays Bank (1921 by Dawson, Son & Allardyce) and Chronicle House (1923 by Ellis & Clarke), was demolished in 2022 despite a 2020 certificate of immunity from listing.

JUNCTION WITH SALISBURY COURT Reuters & Press Association Building (No.82–85) Lutyens’ stripped Classical style is enlivened by features including a double-height entrance arch and concave attic storeys. Built 1934–38. Grade II listed. (claxity.com) Birmingham Post (No.88) Edwardian newspaper office with applied ornament and good proportions. Built in 1900 by Belcher & Joass.

Brick with second-storey stone facing and an 1897 Art Nouveau numeral plaque.

St Bartholomew House (No.90–94) T udoresque brick with sandstone dressings, bay and arched windows, loggia and gabled roof. Good ornamental carvings along the ground-floor frontages. Built in 1900 by H. Huntley Gordon. Grade II listed.

The Old Bell (No.95) & No.96 Simple low-rise buildings of probable C17 origin with 1897 façades. The pub occupies No.95. Grade II listed.

Workmanlike late Victorian brick building with a canted corner.

The Punch Tavern (No.98–100) Victorian brick façade with stone dressings, distinguished by finely carved window surrounds, articulated architraves and richly tiled entrance. The ornate interiors include a well-preserved hammerbeam skylight, making the Neo- Jacobean design especially successful. Built in 1894 by Saville & Martin. Grade II listed.

Brick commercial premises with stone detailing. Late C19 refacing of a former cigar and snuff manufacturer’s premises.
