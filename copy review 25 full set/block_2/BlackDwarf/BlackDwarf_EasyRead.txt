In 1816, William Cobbett started the Political Register, a weekly paper written as essays to avoid the newspaper tax. It was cheap, easy to read, and became very popular, reaching 70,000 readers by 1817. Cobbett had been imprisoned before for criticizing the government and fled to America when Habeas Corpus was suspended.
His example inspired other radical newspapers. Thomas Wooler started the Black Dwarf in 1817, using satire, parodies, and clear writing to attack the government and support reform. Wooler faced legal trouble but continued publishing with help from Richard Carlile. The paper closed in 1824, with Wooler admitting he had overestimated public support for political reform.
