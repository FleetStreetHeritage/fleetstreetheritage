From 1882 to 1890, Charles Bradlaugh MP and Annie Besant ran the Freethought Publishing Company at 63 Fleet Street. The company republished a controversial birth control pamphlet, Fruits of Philosophy, to help working people control family size and challenge censorship. The pamphlet sold extremely well, and Bradlaugh and Besant were prosecuted but won on appeal.
Bradlaugh later became MP for Northampton and fought to take his seat without a religious oath, helping pass the Oaths Act 1888. Besant became famous for the 1888 Match Girls’ Strike and later moved to India, campaigning for Indian Home Rule.
The Freethought Publishing Company also published radical works on women’s rights, republicanism, and social reform, making it a key center for London’s radical ideas.
