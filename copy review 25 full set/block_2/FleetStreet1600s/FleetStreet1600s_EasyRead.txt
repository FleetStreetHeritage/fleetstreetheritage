In the 1600s, Fleet Street was busy, dangerous, and full of life. Builders, printers, lawyers, and noblemen filled the area, and it was constantly being rebuilt, especially after the Great Fire of 1666. New shops, churches, and public buildings appeared, and even women ran businesses like coffee houses.
The street was dirty and risky. The Fleet River was an open sewer, crime was common, and disease like the plague was deadly. Winters were harsh, and coal smoke made the air thick and black.
Fleet Street was also exciting. It was the main route between Westminster and the City, so you could see coronation processions, executions, and public events. The Civil War brought lots of political pamphlets, and women played an active role in petitions and protests.
For fun, there were inns, taverns, and theatres. You could meet famous visitors, see exotic animals, or watch plays at places like the Salisbury Court Theatre. Some historic sites, like the Old Cheshire Cheese pub, still let you imagine life in seventeenth-century Fleet Street.
