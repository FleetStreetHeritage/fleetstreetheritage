The Children’s Newspaper was launched in 1919 by Arthur Mee and ran until his death in 1943. Subtitled The Story of the World Today for the Men and Women of Tomorrow, it aimed to educate children using Christian values and a sense of British pride. Mee believed that teaching children about the world could guide them to better, more creative lives.
The first issue included news, science discoveries, and long-running columns like Ella Wheeler Wilcox’s letters to girls. Over time, the paper covered sports, television, and interviews, keeping young readers up to date with the world.
Arthur Mee (1875–1943) rose from humble beginnings in Nottingham to become a leading journalist. He started as a copy-holder and reporter, taught himself shorthand, and worked for the Daily Mail. He created successful publications like the Harmsworth History of the World and the Children’s Encyclopaedia, which taught children about science, history, geography, and practical skills.
Mee’s biggest achievement was presenting knowledge in a way children could understand, especially those who had only basic education after leaving school at 14. The Children’s Newspaper reflected his lifelong mission: to educate, inform, and inspire the next generation.
