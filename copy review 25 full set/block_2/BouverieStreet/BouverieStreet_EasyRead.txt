Bouverie Street runs off the west end of Fleet Street, sloping steeply toward the Thames. It didn’t exist in the Middle Ages; the area was the boundary between the Whitefriars Monastery and Templar land. After the Reformation, the land changed hands, and around 1800 Jacob, 2nd Earl Radnor, laid out the street, naming it after his family.
Originally, the street had many small businesses, including solicitors, printers, florists, and hotels. By the early 20th century, newspapers and publishers had taken over, including offices for the Daily Mirror, Daily News, Star, Punch, and News of the World.
Today, much of Bouverie Street has been redeveloped. The Fleet Street Heritage Sundial is painted on a wall at the entrance, and many old office buildings have been replaced or modernized. Current occupiers include Euromoney plc, the Polish consulate, and Ashfield Healthcare.
