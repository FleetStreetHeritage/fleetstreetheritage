The River Fleet is only five miles long, running from Hampstead to the Thames near Blackfriars. The area around Farringdon had the Fleet Telephone Exchange and old oyster tubs. The riverbanks had prisons like Ludgate, Newgate, and Fleet Prison. Fleet Prison was famous for holding notable prisoners and for “Fleet Marriages” until 1753.
Much of the river was later covered. Ludgate Circus was an old crossing, replaced by bridges built by Wren. New Bridge Street covers part of the river. Nearby are the holy well of St Bride, Bridewell Palace, and the old prison gatehouse. Whitefriars and Blackfriars Monasteries once dominated the area, later becoming semi-independent zones called Alsatia.
At the river’s mouth, the Black Friar pub marks Blackfriars Monastery, later used for theatre by Shakespeare’s company. The Fleet now flows underground in Bazalgette’s sewers, beneath Victoria Embankment. Storm drains and hidden tunnels still carry the river water today.
