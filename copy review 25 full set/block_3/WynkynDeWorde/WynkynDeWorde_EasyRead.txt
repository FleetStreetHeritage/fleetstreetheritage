Wynkyn de Worde was an important English printer and publisher in the late 1400s and early 1500s. He came from the Rhineland and moved to London in 1476 to work for William Caxton, who brought the printing press to England. After Caxton died in 1491, de Worde took over the business and moved near Fleet Street to reach more customers, including merchants, the Court, and the Church.
De Worde focused on printing books that were affordable and popular, such as religious works, poetry, children’s books, and household guides. He added illustrations to make his books more appealing and printed over six hundred titles in his career. He also helped standardize English spelling and grammar and printed famous works like Chaucer’s Canterbury Tales and Malory’s Morte D’Arthur.
He was an innovator, using italic type, English-made paper, and movable type for music. His business grew, and he became influential in the early printing trade and the Stationers’ Company. Wynkyn de Worde worked into his seventies, died in 1535, and was buried at St Bride’s Church. Today, the Wynkyn de Worde Society honors his contribution to printing.
