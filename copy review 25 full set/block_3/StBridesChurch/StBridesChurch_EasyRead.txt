St Bride’s Church, known as the Journalists’ Church and formerly the Printers’ Church, has nearly 2,000 years of history. In its crypts, visitors can see foundations of seven earlier buildings, from the 6th to the 15th centuries, as well as Roman paving and artifacts from AD 180. The Portland stone exterior contrasts with the bright, peaceful interior, offering a quiet sanctuary away from the busy streets of Fleet Street.
The current church was designed by Sir Christopher Wren and completed in 1675 after the medieval church was destroyed in the Great Fire of London. It was damaged again in the 1940 Blitz, but with the help of the Fleet Street press, architect Godfrey Allen, and Revd Cyril Armitage, the church was rebuilt in 1957. The restoration followed Wren’s original design for the structure, while the interior furnishings were redesigned with oak carvings, decorative ceilings, and artwork including a trompe l’oeil celestial choir. The tower and spire inspired the design of the first multi-tiered wedding cake.
St Bride’s also revived its historic Guild, giving a sense of purpose to its members, and established a professional choir that still performs at services, weddings, and memorials. Today, the church welcomes everyone as a place of worship, prayer, or quiet reflection, offering a calm retreat in the heart of the city.
