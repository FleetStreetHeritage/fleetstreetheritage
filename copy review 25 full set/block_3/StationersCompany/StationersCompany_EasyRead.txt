Stationers’ Hall is near Fleet Street and has been there for about 350 years. The Stationers’ Company is much older, founded in 1403 by people who made, bound, and sold books.
The Company became powerful after the printing press arrived in England. In 1557, it received a Royal Charter, giving it control over book publishing. Printers had to get permission to publish a book and register it at Stationers’ Hall to gain the exclusive right to print it.
Two important printers worked on Fleet Street. Robert Copland ran a print shop and wrote an early English poem about poverty. Richard Tottell published legal books and, in 1557, printed the first English poetry collection, later called Tottel’s Miscellany.
Fleet Street remained important for publishing. In 1702, Elizabeth Mallet published Britain’s first daily newspaper. In 1739, Samuel Richardson wrote the first English novel, Pamela, at his printing house.
The Stationers’ Company still exists today as the Worshipful Company of Stationers and Newspaper Makers, continuing its long connection with Fleet Street.
