Sweeney Todd, the “demon barber of Fleet Street,” first appeared in 1846 in the serialized story The String of Pearls. The story follows Todd, a barber near St Dunstan-in-the-West, who murders his customers and helps Mrs Lovett turn them into meat pies. The plot centers on Lieutenant Thornhill’s stolen necklace and the efforts of Johanna, Tobias, and others to uncover Todd’s crimes. In the end, Todd is hanged and Mrs Lovett dies of poison, rewarding the heroes and punishing the villains.
Although fictional, Todd’s barbershop is imagined to be on Fleet Street, likely near number 186 or the current site of the Old Bank pub. The story was written in a simple, sensational style typical of “penny bloods,” and its popularity spread through oral storytelling and dramatic adaptations. It reflects the anxieties of early 19th-century London life, with its crowded streets, industrial hardships, and sense of urban danger, which made Todd’s grisly crimes both terrifying and fascinating to readers.
The tale’s enduring appeal comes from its mix of horror, suspense, and social commentary, helping Sweeney Todd become a lasting figure in literature, theatre, and film.
