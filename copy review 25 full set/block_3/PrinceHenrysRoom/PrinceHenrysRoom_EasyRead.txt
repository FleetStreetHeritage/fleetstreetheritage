17 Fleet Street is one of the few buildings in the City of London to survive the Great Fire of 1666. Its first floor has a large room with fine wooden panelling and a unique Jacobean plaster ceiling featuring the Prince of Wales’ feathers.
The site was part of the Knights Templar estate from the 12th century, later passing to the Knights Hospitallers, who leased it to law students. The front section became the Prince’s Arms inn in the 16th century and may have been used by Henry, Prince of Wales, though this is uncertain. The building also housed Mrs Salmon’s Waxworks until around 1816.
In 1900, the London County Council saved the building from demolition and restored it, preserving the historic first-floor room. Original features, including a half-timbered front and the plaster ceiling, were uncovered during restoration. Stained glass windows were added later.
The building changed hands to the Greater London Council and then the City of London Corporation in 1969 and is not usually open to the public.
