The Manchester Guardian was founded in 1821 by John Taylor and friends, soon after the Peterloo Massacre. The paper promised to support civil and religious liberty and reform. It started as a weekly, became a daily in 1855, and opposed slavery, supported free trade, and criticized parts of the American Civil War.
CP Scott became editor in 1872 and shaped the paper’s values, keeping it independent and saying, “Comment is free, but facts are sacred.” In 1936, ownership passed to the Scott Trust to protect its independence. The paper, nicknamed the Grauniad for its quirky style, was renamed The Guardian in 1959 and continues its radical, independent tradition today.
