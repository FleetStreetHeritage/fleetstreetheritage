Picture Post started on 1 October 1938 and was different from older illustrated papers because it aimed at all readers, not just the rich. Using photo-journalism, it told stories with pictures, and quickly sold over a million copies.
The paper was created by Stefan Lorent (editor), Tom Hopkinson (assistant editor), and Edward Hulton (owner/funder). It was liberal, anti-Fascist, and campaigned against the persecution of Jews in Germany. In 1941, it proposed ideas like a minimum wage, full employment, and a national health service.
Lorent left in 1940, Hopkinson became editor, and circulation peaked at nearly two million. Tensions with the conservative owner led to Hopkinson’s dismissal in 1950, and the paper closed in 1957. Its photos are now in the Getty archive.
