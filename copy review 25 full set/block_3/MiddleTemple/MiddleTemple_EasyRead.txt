The Honourable Society of the Middle Temple is one of the four Inns of Court in London and has the right to call barristers to the Bar of England and Wales. It supports and educates barristers at all stages of their careers.
The Middle Temple began in the mid-1300s on land once owned by the Knights Templar. By the 1400s, it had become a respected legal institution training students through study, practice, and shared dining. Its location near the City of London and Westminster made it an important social and educational centre.
A new Hall was built in 1573 and became the heart of the Inn. Although the Inn declined during and after the English Civil War, legal education was revived in the 1800s. In 1919, Middle Temple admitted its first female student, Helena Normanton.
The Inn was badly damaged in World War II but restored by 1949. Today, Middle Temple continues its long tradition of legal education, with Hall still central to its life and activities.
