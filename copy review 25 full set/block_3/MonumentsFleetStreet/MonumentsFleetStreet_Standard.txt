The church of St. Dunstan-in-the-West at the western end of Fleet Street has  three monuments on its facade. The statue of Queen Elizabeth I originally stood on Lud-gate, which was reconstructed during her reign. It has a barely-legible date of 1586 at the base. Ludgate was demolished in 1760 and the statue was then placed here.  It is believed to be the oldest public statue in London. To Dunstan-in-the-West and above it to the left of the statue are Gog and Magog, the legendary guardians of the City, poised with hammers to ring out the bells every hour. The clock was extensively restored around 2018. Below the clock, at street level, is a sculpted head of Lord Northcliffe, a press baron, who gave benefactions to the Church.
