Selim “Eddy” Shah, born in Cambridge in 1944, revolutionised the British newspaper industry. After early work in theatre and television, he started his own local newspaper, embracing modern computer technology instead of traditional hot-metal printing. In 1983, he famously challenged the print unions, refusing to reinstate striking staff, sparking mass picketing and court battles.
In 1986, Shah launched Today, the first colour national newspaper produced without union interference, pioneering electronic production. Although Today was not commercially lasting, it forced all UK national newspapers to modernise, leading to the collapse of Fleet Street’s traditional print unions and transforming the industry. His courage and innovation earned him recognition as a central figure in Britain’s media revolution.
