The Knights Templar came to England in 1128. They first settled near Chancery Lane, then moved to the Temple near Fleet Street in 1184, where they built the Temple Church. They also owned land for jousting, horse training, and armour-making. The Templars were a military religious order founded in 1109 to protect pilgrims in Jerusalem. Members lived by strict rules of poverty, chastity, and obedience. Only a few were knights, while others worked in supporting roles.
The Templars became very wealthy, owning land and developing early banking and trade. After Jerusalem and Acre fell, their mission ended. In the early 1300s, King Philip IV of France arrested many, tortured some, and executed others. The Pope then dissolved the Order and gave their property to the Knights Hospitallers.
In England, they left a lasting mark. Many places are named Temple, like Temple Meads, and important sites include Baldock, Denny Abbey, and the Temple Church, which still has knight effigies.
