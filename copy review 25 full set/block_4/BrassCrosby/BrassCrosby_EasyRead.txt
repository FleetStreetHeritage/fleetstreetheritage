Brass Crosby was born in Stockton in 1725 and trained as a lawyer before rising through London’s civic ranks to become Lord Mayor in 1770. As Chief Magistrate, he stood up to naval press gangs and famously defended a printer who published the proceedings of Parliament, refusing to punish him. For this, Crosby was imprisoned in the Tower of London, sparking nationwide protests. After six weeks, he was released to great celebration. His courage ensured the freedom of the press and ended the use of “general” warrants. Crosby married in 1772, lived in Chatham Place, and died in 1793, commemorated by an obelisk at St George’s Circus.
