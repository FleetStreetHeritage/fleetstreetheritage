

The Guardian has always tried to change with the times while keeping its main values. In 1959, it stopped being a regional paper and became a national one based in London. Its editors often took unpopular but principled positions, even when this caused political criticism or financial problems.
From the 1970s onwards, the paper modernised to attract new readers. It improved its style, focused more on investigations, and later used the internet to reach readers around the world. Big stories helped make the Guardian internationally well known, and it began to rely more on support from its readers.
In recent years, the Guardian has focused on fairness, diversity, and the environment. It has admitted mistakes from its past and taken clear moral positions, such as refusing gambling adverts. Today, the paper continues to put its values before profit.
