Child’s Bank (No. 1) A grand Renaissance-style building with detailed Portland Stone, giant Corinthian columns, and arched ground-floor openings. Built in 1878 by John Gibson, now owned by NatWest. Grade II* listed.
S. Weingott & Son (No. 3) Refaced in 1912 for a tobacconist shop, featuring large metal-framed windows and simple stonework. Grade II listed.
Middle Temple Gateway Built in 1684, this elegant red brick and stone entrance has Ionic columns, balconies, and a dentilled pediment. Replaces a 1520 structure. Grade I listed.
Legal & General Assurance (No. 10) A brick and terracotta building in a loose Flemish style, with an asymmetric design. Built in 1885 by RW Edis. Grade II listed.
Union Bank of London (No. 13-14) Neoclassical stone building with Doric columns on the ground floor. Built in 1856 by G Aitchison Sr. Grade II listed.
Wildy & Sons (No. 15-16) Narrow but ornate frontage from 1856, featuring a decorative Venetian-style window.
Inner Temple Gateway / Prince Henry’s Room (No. 17) Half-timbered building from 1610-11, restored in 1900-06. Preserved character, used as pub, wax museum, etc. Grade II* listed.
Goslings & Sharpe (No. 18-19) Renaissance Revival style with Neoclassical features, now a Barclays bank. Built in 1898 by AC Blomfield. Grade II listed.
London & Provincial Life Assurance (No. 21) Italianate Renaissance Revival style with tall, narrow frontage and carved details. Built in 1853 by J Shaw Jr. Grade II listed.
Ye Olde Cock Tavern (No. 22) Tudor Revival building from 1912, replacing an earlier 1888 structure. Grade II listed.
Temple Bar House (No. 23-28) Classic style with Baroque details, granite on the ground floor, and solid upper-storey design. Built in 1902 by Joseph, Son & Smithem.
Promoter Insurance (No. 29) High Victorian eclectic style with varied storeys and arched attic windows. Built in 1860 by WG Bartleet. Grade II listed.
Messrs Philip (No. 30-32) Portland stone building with decorative windows and historicist details. Built in 1883 by TE Knightley. Grade II listed.
No. 33 Plain Georgian brick building from the 18th century, former home of Murray’s Publishers. Grade II listed.
Hoare’s Bank (No. 37) Refined sandstone building with elegant interiors. Built in 1829 by C Parker, still a private bank. Grade II* listed.
No. 40-43 Neoclassical façade with Ionic pilasters and a strong base. Built in 1913 by OC Wylson.
Mitre House (No. 44-45) Stripped Classical style with Art Deco lettering. Built in 1929 by MK Matthews.
London News Agency (No. 46) Simple brick building with large windows, former photographic archive office (1908-1972).
El Vino wine bar (No. 47) Edwardian brick building with original signage and preserved interiors. Inspired a fictional bar in Rumpole of the Bailey.
Norwich Union Insurance (No. 49-50) Grand Edwardian Renaissance Revival with arches, colonnade, and palatial upper floors. Built 1911-12 by JM Brooks. Grade II listed.
No. 53 Narrow, colorful façade with red and green patterned brick and Gothic bay windows. Circa 1905.
Clifford Milburn & Co. (No. 54) Vertical design with chamfered uprights and simple aprons. Built in 1927 by Trehearne & Norman.
No. 55 Brick and stone building with classic features like pilasters and arches. Built in the 1930s.
