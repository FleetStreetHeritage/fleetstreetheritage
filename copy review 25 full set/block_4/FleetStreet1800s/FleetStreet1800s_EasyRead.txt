Important events of the 19th century included the Battle of Trafalgar (1805), the Peterloo Massacre (1819), the opening of the Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830), the Great Reform Bill (1832), the first London Underground (1863), and Queen Victoria’s death (1901).
Problems with the Fleet River grew over time. The Holborn Bridge had steep hills, so carts avoided it, while the Fleet Bridge was easier to use. The river silted up, and expensive dredging by Wren and Hooke in the 1670s only partly helped. By 1736, the river was bricked over and the Fleet Market built along its line.
To the south, Alsatia, or the “liberty of the Fleet,” was outside the law and popular for instant marriages. London’s growing population required more food, expanding Smithfield Market to five acres, handling tens of thousands of cattle and sheep.
Roads and turnpikes improved, stagecoaches made travel faster, and William Dockra’s Penny Post (1680) sped up letter delivery, helping business and newspapers thrive.
