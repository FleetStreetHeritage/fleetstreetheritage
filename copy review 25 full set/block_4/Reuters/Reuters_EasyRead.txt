
On 14 June 1851, Paul Julius Reuter, a German immigrant, arrived in London to set up a news and financial information service. He had previously used telegraph cables and carrier pigeons to send stock prices quickly and accurately. Reuter opened his office at No. 1 Royal Exchange in October 1851, just before the first undersea telegraph cable between Dover and Calais became operational.
Reuter expanded his service across Europe, providing financial news and later general news to newspapers. He became known for speed, accuracy, and impartiality, with even Queen Victoria as a customer. Reuter pioneered new technology, building private telegraph lines, using early teleprinters, and later radio and satellite transmissions to distribute news worldwide.
In the 1960s and 1970s, Reuters embraced computerized financial services, offering real-time market data and currency exchange rates globally. The company grew into a multimedia news organization, later merging with Thomson Corporation in 2008 to form Thomson Reuters. Today, it continues to deliver news quickly, including breaking international events.
