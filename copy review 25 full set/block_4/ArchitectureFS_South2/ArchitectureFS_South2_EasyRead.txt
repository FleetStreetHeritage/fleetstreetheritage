Glasgow Herald (No. 56-57) Tall, narrow building with post-Classical style and Art Deco details. Granite and gilt on the ground floor, with a 3-storey bow window above. Built 1927 by Tubbs, Son & Duncan. Grade II listed.
No. 58-59 Late 19th or early 20th-century buildings in traditional styles. No. 58 has standout Queen Anne details. Now combined as a single building.
No. 60 Associated with 18th-century clockmaker George Adams. Upper floors keep Georgian style but the building looks more modern.
J Lyons Café (No. 61) Edwardian Baroque Revival style building, formerly part of Lyons’ tea shop chain. Built 1910, likely by Lyons’ in-house architects.
Bouverie Street – The Scotsman (No. 63) Postwar stripped Classicism with a prominent cornice and wrought iron balconies. Built 1921 by Frank Matcham.
No. 64-65 Asymmetric Post-modern design with a large arch and courtyard. Dark lower floors, lighter middle, and recessed attic. Built 1988 by YRM Partnership.
The Tipperary (No. 66) Rebuilt after a fire, with 1895 façade and interiors. Grade II listed.
No. 67 Rounded corner with simple cladding over four storeys plus attic. Built 1926 by AAH Scott.
Whitefriars Street Buildings including Barclays Bank (1921) and Chronicle House (1923) were demolished in 2022. A 2020 immunity certificate prevented their historic listing.
Salisbury Court – Reuters & Press Association (No. 82-85) Lutyens’ postwar classicism with double-storey entrance arch and concave attic. Built 1934-1938. Grade II listed.
Birmingham Post (No. 88) Edwardian newspaper office with ornamentation and well-proportioned design. Built 1900 by Belcher & Joass.
No. 89 Brick building with stone facing on the 2nd storey and Art Nouveau date plaque from 1897.
St Bartholomew House (No. 90-94) Tudoresque brick with sandstone details, bay and arched windows, loggia, and gabled roof. Built 1900 by H Huntley Gordon. Grade II listed.
The Old Bell (No. 95-96) Simple low-rise buildings, probably 17th century, with 1897 façade. Pub at No. 95. Upper floors are worn. Grade II listed.
No. 97 Late Victorian brick building with a canted corner.
The Punch Tavern (No. 98-100) Neo-Jacobean brick with stone details, richly carved windows, polychrome entrance tiles, and ornate interiors. Built 1894 by Saville & Martin. Grade II listed.
No. 101 Brick commercial building with stone details. Late 19th-century refacing of a cigar and snuff manufacturer’s premises.
