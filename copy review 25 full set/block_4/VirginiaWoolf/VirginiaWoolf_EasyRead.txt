
Virginia Woolf, born in 1882, was one of the most important twentieth-century authors and a member of the Bloomsbury Group. She is best known for novels written in a stream-of-consciousness style, such as Mrs Dalloway (1925) and To the Lighthouse (1927).
In 1912, she married Leonard Woolf. At the time, she lived in Bloomsbury with her brother Adrian, while Leonard had a room on the fourth floor—an unusual arrangement for the period. After their honeymoon, they moved to rooms at Cliffords Inn off Fleet Street, a decayed Inn of Chancery.
The move allowed them more time together and space for writing. Virginia kept detailed diaries and letters, giving a vivid view of their daily life, friendships, and home. In 1913, a recurrence of her mental illness forced them to leave Cliffords Inn. They eventually moved to Richmond, settling at Hogarth House, where they continued their work and writing.
