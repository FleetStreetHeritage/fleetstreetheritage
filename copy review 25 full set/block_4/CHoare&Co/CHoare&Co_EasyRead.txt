Hoare’s Bank, trading under the Golden Bottle for over 350 years, is a famous part of old Fleet Street. Richard Hoare, a goldsmith, started the bank in 1672 and moved it to Fleet Street in 1690 after the Great Fire. Hoare served wealthy clients like Samuel Pepys and Catherine of Braganza, lending only to people he trusted. He became wealthy, was knighted in 1702, and later served as Lord Mayor of London.
The Hoare family were also pioneers in charity. In 1719, Henry Hoare co-founded Westminster Hospital, the first fully public-funded hospital. Later generations supported schools, churches, and hospitals, including King’s College Hospital and Trinity Hospice.
The Fleet Street bank building has been rebuilt and updated over the years, surviving events like Queen Victoria’s Jubilee in 1897 and a near-destruction in the Blitz of 1941. Today, Hoare’s Bank remains privately owned, run by the founder’s descendants, and continues to lend carefully while supporting philanthropy. The original golden bottle, symbol of Richard Hoare’s vision, is kept in the bank museum.
