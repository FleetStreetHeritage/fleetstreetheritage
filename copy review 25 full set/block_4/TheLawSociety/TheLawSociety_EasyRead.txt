The Law Society has welcomed people to its Grade II* listed building at 113 Chancery Lane since 1831. It is the professional body for solicitors in England and Wales, promoting and protecting solicitors, the rule of law, and justice. Its motto, “We preserve the laws and legal rights,” guides its work, from supporting free legal advice and administering Legal Aid after 1945, to encouraging diversity through its scholarship-based Diversity Access Scheme.
The Society was founded in 1825 by a group of solicitors led by Bryan Holme, initially called the Society of Attorneys, Solicitors, Proctors and others, and began with 292 members. Lewis Vulliamy, a pupil of Sir Robert Smirke, designed the Hall, inspired by classical architecture and his travels in Europe. The building has expanded over time, with contributions from architects such as Charles Holden and Philip Hardwick, and today includes a library, reading room, common room, and even a restaurant called Six Clerks, named after the historic office once on the site.
