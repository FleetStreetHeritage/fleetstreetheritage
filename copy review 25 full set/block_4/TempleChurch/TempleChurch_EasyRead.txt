Temple Church in London is very old and beautiful. It was built by the Knights Templar, a group of monks who protected pilgrims. The Round Church, finished in 1185, was modelled on a church in Jerusalem. Inside are important statues, including William Marshal, who helped King John and whose family was linked to Magna Carta.
The Chancel was built in 1240, possibly for Henry III. After the Templars were banned, lawyers from the Inner and Middle Temple moved in. In the 1500s, a famous religious debate happened here, leading to an important book about the Church by Richard Hooker. In 1608, King James I gave the land to the Inns of Court, who must look after the church.
Sir Christopher Wren updated the church in the 1670s. It was badly damaged in World War II but was fixed 17 years later. Today, Temple Church is still used for worship, has a fine choir, and hosts concerts and talks. It is a quiet and peaceful place in the busy City of London.
