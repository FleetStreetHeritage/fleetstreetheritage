Sweeney Todd, the “demon barber of Fleet Street,” first appeared in 1846 in a story called The String of Pearls, published by Edward Lloyd at 12 Salisbury Square. The story was part of cheap weekly serials called “Penny Bloods,” which were very popular with working-class readers and helped more people learn to read before schools were widely available.
Salisbury Square had a long history of books and entertainment. From 1671 to 1709, the Dorset Garden Theatre put on big plays there, and in 1740 the famous novel Pamela was published from the square. By the 1800s, Edward Lloyd ran his newspapers there, making it an important place for publishing and popular stories.
Most of Salisbury Square was destroyed in the 1940 air raids, though some buildings lasted until later. In 2021, almost everything left was knocked down, upsetting heritage groups. The only reminder of the past is an obelisk to Robert Waithman, a former Mayor of London, which is in storage but will be returned in the new Salisbury Square “Justice Quarter.”
