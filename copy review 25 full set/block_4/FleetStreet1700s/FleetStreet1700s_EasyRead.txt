Important events in 18th-century London included the publication of Britain’s first daily newspaper, the Daily Courant, in 1702, and the completion of St. Paul’s Cathedral in 1710. Meanwhile, problems with the Fleet River grew. Its two bridges had steep approaches, and the river below the Fleet Bridge had silted up. Christopher Wren and Robert Hooke supervised dredging in the 1670s, but silting continued. By 1736, the river was bricked over and the Fleet Market built along its line.
To the south of Fleet Street lay Alsatia, or the “liberty of the Fleet,” a law-free zone where instant marriages were common. London’s growing population required more food, leading to the expansion of Smithfield Market to five acres, handling tens of thousands of cattle and sheep.
Improved roads and turnpikes, along with the Penny Post introduced by William Dockra in 1680, made travel and communication faster. Stagecoach routes reduced Edinburgh–London travel from 10–14 days to 3–4 days a century later, boosting business, literacy, and newspaper circulation.
