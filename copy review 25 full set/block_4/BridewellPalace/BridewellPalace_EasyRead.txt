Bridewell Palace has had many roles over 500 years: royal residence, prison, school, and hospital. Today, only a replica gatehouse on New Bridge Street remains.
Built in 1510 and gifted to Henry VIII by Cardinal Wolsey in 1515, the palace was named after St Bride’s Church and a holy spring beneath it. It featured grand courtyards, gardens, and a processional staircase, later copied in other palaces like Hampton Court.
Bridewell was the site of Henry VIII’s efforts to annul his marriage to Catherine of Aragon and later hosted French ambassadors, inspiring Holbein’s famous painting The Ambassadors.
In 1553, Edward VI turned it into a prison, later adding a hospital. By 1921, the remaining land became the offices of Lever Bros, later Unilever, showing how this historic palace evolved with the city.
