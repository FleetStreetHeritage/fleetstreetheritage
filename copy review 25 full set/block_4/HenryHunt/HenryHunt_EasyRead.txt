Henry Hunt (1773–1835) was a tall, strong man from Wiltshire who became a famous speaker and political reformer. Early troubles, including a short prison sentence and personal scandals, pushed him into public life. Known as “Orator” Hunt, he used his powerful voice to campaign for fairness, transparency, and social justice. In London, he exposed corruption in the City, demanded public accounts, criticized high salaries and waste, and worked to protect ordinary citizens from abuse.
Hunt also tried to serve in City office, often clashing with the powerful Tory leader Robert Waithman. He became an MP for Preston in 1830 and was involved in the movement for parliamentary reform. He supported efforts to improve conditions for the poor and even presented the first petition for women’s right to vote to Parliament in 1832, though it was rejected.
Despite setbacks and opposition, Hunt remained committed to reform until his health declined. He died in 1835 at Alresford. He is remembered as a passionate campaigner who fought for fairness, honesty in government, and the rights of ordinary people.
