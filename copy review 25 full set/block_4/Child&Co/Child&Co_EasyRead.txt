

Child & Co. was a famous private bank located at 1 Fleet Street in London, founded in the 17th century. It began as a goldsmith’s business run by Robert Blanchard, later joined by Francis Child. Under Francis Child’s leadership, the bank grew wealthy and respected. He was knighted, worked for King William III, and regularly lent money to the government. The bank also built strong relationships with lawyers in the Temple and Oxford colleges, which helped it survive several financial crises.
The bank remained a family business for many generations. In the late 1700s, Francis Child’s descendant Sarah Anne Child famously ran away to marry the Earl of Westmoreland after her father refused permission. Although she was disinherited, the bank and fortune passed to her daughter, Sarah Sophia. She later became Lady Jersey and inherited the bank in 1806, remaining its senior partner until 1867. She rarely visited the bank but took a strong interest in how it was run.
During the 1800s, the bank rebuilt its premises and continued operating despite increasing competition from larger banks. In 1924, it was sold to another banking firm. After introducing modern services in the mid-20th century, Child & Co. finally closed in 2022, ending over 350 years of banking history.
