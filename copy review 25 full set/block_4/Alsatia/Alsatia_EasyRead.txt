From the 1670s to 1697, the area between Fleet Street and the Thames was called Alsatia. It included Whitefriars, Salisbury Court, Ram Alley, and Mitre Court. Across the river in Southwark were places like The Mint and The Clink. Alsatia was named after Alsace in France and became a safe haven for debtors avoiding prison.
Since 1352, people who could not pay debts could be jailed, often in poor and unsafe conditions, and had to pay for their own food. Some churches and special liberties, like Whitefriars, allowed debtors to claim sanctuary. The area also became known for theatres and appeared in plays like Thomas Shadwell’s The Squire of Alsatia, where its residents were shown as rogues and imposters.
In 1691, a riot broke out against Temple lawyers. The King’s Guard restored order, and Captain Francis Winter was executed outside Whitefriars. After this, the government ended such privileges with the 1697 Escape from Prisons Act, abolishing Alsatia and other sanctuary areas.
